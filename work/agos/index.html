The brief

While I was a UI/UX designer at an agency, I designed this homepage as a proposal for a long-established plumbing and fire protection company. It had to do two jobs: show decades of trust to building owners and contractors, and lead them to the products, the services and a quote. The proposal wasn’t chosen.

For the portfolio I kept the design and replaced the client’s identity. Agos (Tagalog for “current” or “flow”) is a fictional company, with a new logo, founder, history and placeholder contact details.

The design

  • One blue, used with intent. Navy for headlines, a bright blue for the accents, buttons and stats, and plenty of white and light grey around them.
  • A V in the hero. The headline sits above a wide photo cut with a shallow V, so the buttons sit in the notch and the photo points the eye toward them.
  • Products first. Four product families sit on soft grey cards before the services, because many visitors come to buy parts.
  • A raised panel. Services and the company story share one rounded white panel with a soft shadow, as if laid over the page.
  • A personal close. The About section is written as a letter from the founder, signed, with the company’s numbers floating beside it as blue bubbles.

Building it from the design

I built the page against its Figma frame. Every measurement is a Figma pixel multiplied by a unit tied to the window width, so at full width the page matches the design section by section. It’s plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with GSAP and Lenis for motion and scrolling.

  • Blends from Figma. The contact banner photo sits in soft light over navy, as in the design, so the pipes read as a deep blue texture.
  • Working basics. The navigation highlights the section you’re reading, the newsletter form checks the email address, and the other links explain that they’re part of a demo.
  • Mobile. The design has no mobile frame, so I laid one out: products two by two, the stats in a row of bubbles, and a full-screen menu.

Motion

  • The headline rises word by word, then the hero photo opens from a sliver in the middle and the V drops in.
  • Scrolling into the photo widens it to the full width of the screen and flattens the V.
  • Products drop onto their cards and float gently; hovering lifts and tilts them over a blue wash.
  • The services and About panel lifts over the page as it arrives, and the service photos open upward.
  • The founder’s portrait rises out of the panel, the signature writes itself, and the stats count up and drift like they’re floating on water.
  • The contact banner opens out as it arrives, its photo drifts behind the copy, and its buttons lean toward the pointer.
