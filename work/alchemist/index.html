About
An online store for Alchemist, a supplements and sportswear brand. I designed it as a paid proposal for a developer's client; it wasn't built then, so I've now built it myself, from the homepage through shop, product page, cart and checkout, with GSAP motion and a WebGL image transition.

The brief

A former colleague, a developer, brought me in as the designer on a side project: a new online store for Alchemist, a brand selling supplements, sportswear and gym accessories. I was the only designer, from research and competitor analysis through wireframes, the UI kit, the desktop and mobile layouts, client revisions and the handoff to the developer. The client decided not to go ahead with development, so the design stayed in Figma until now.

All the photography, product shots, posters and partner logos come from the brand.

The design

The store had to feel like a training brand, not a pharmacy. Headlines are set in heavy, slanted small caps, and the layout is black, white and grey, so the products carry the colour. Section titles and buttons share the same hard edges, and buttons have a grey bar on one side, like a gym tag.

The homepage is long because it does a lot of selling:

  • a sale announcement bar and a hero carousel
  • delivery and payment promises
  • promo cards and category shortcuts
  • the brand story and video
  • product shelves for supplements, sportswear and accessories
  • bundles, partner gyms, the brand’s blog, and a full footer

Behind it sits the rest of the shopping flow: a shop page with category filters, a product page, the cart, checkout and a contact page, plus a Sportswear menu that drops down from the header.

Building it from the design

I built every page against its Figma frame. Every measurement is a Figma pixel multiplied by a unit tied to the window width, so at full width each page matches the design section by section.

A few things took care:

  • The headline font. The design uses Aspire, which isn’t licensed for the web. I matched it with Saira Black Italic set in small caps, which keeps the taller capitals that give the headlines their shape.
  • Product images. Several photos are masked, cropped or colour-shifted inside Figma: one shirt photo becomes seven coloured tees, one pair of shorts becomes tan and brown. I exported those exactly as Figma renders them rather than rebuilding the effects in CSS.
  • One catalogue. All 26 products live in one small data file, so the shop, product page, cart and checkout always agree on names, photos and prices.
  • Placeholder copy. Every product in the homepage file was named “A1 Creatine Monohydrate”, and sale prices showed the same number twice. In the build, each product carries the name of what’s pictured, and a “-20%” item really is 20% off.

Motion and the shader

The hero carousel changes slides with a hand-written WebGL shader: a slanted, slash-shaped wipe with a rough edge, where the old image slides out, the new one slides in, and the colour channels split briefly along the edge. The product gallery uses the same shader, so changing a photo or a colour feels like part of the same brand. It only runs during a change, and without WebGL the images simply crossfade.

Around it, GSAP and Lenis handle the rest:

  • Headlines snap up letter by letter.
  • Product cards rise in rows, and they drop out and back in when you change a filter.
  • “Add to bag” sends a small copy of the product photo arcing into the bag icon.
  • Cart totals roll to their new number when you change a quantity.
  • On the contact page, the map’s roads draw in and the store pin drops into place.
  • The header steps aside on the way down and returns on the way up.

A store that works

It isn’t a real shop, but it behaves like one:

  • Bag: what you add on any page stays in the bag as you move around the site.
  • Shop: filter by category, new arrivals, best sellers or discounts, search, and page through the results. Links from the menu and footer open the right filter.
  • Product page: choose a size and colour (the photo changes with the colour) and a quantity. It asks for a size before adding.
  • Cart: change quantities, remove items, and see the totals update.
  • Checkout: the form checks each field, and the homepage’s promo code ALCHEMIST takes 30% off. Placing the order shows a confirmation and empties the bag.

Mobile and weight

The homepage only had a desktop frame, so I designed its phone layout in code: product shelves go to two columns, category shortcuts scroll sideways, and the blog becomes a swipeable row. The store pages had phone frames in Figma, and I followed those.

The six pages ship 2.7 MB of WebP in total, against 62 MB of original images for the homepage alone. This is a portfolio demo, not the Alchemist store: nothing can be bought or sent, the bag lives only in your browser, the contact details are placeholders, and the pages are hidden from search engines.
