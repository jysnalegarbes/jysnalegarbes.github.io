The brief

In 2024, at an agency, I designed a homepage proposal for a studio that offers both tattoos and skin aesthetics. The client didn’t go ahead. The design was later used as an onboarding exercise for developer applicants, and I coded a version of it myself while learning front-end.

This year I came back to it as both designer and developer. The content stayed the same; the identity, the structure and the build are new. The studio shown here is fictional, and none of the original client’s name or branding is used.

What the first build got wrong

  • Type that whispered. Every heading used Megrim, a hairline display face. It holds up at 60px and falls apart at the 16px it was also used for.
  • Two businesses, no detail. Tattoos and Aesthetics were two buttons with nothing behind them. Nothing said what either service included.
  • Six identical cards. Each artist got a photo and, on hover, a name. Nothing about their craft.
  • Booking went nowhere. “Book now” scrolled to an email address in the footer.
  • Weight and bugs. 6 MB of PNGs, a mobile menu that attached its click handler twice, and a footer placed outside the page body.

Direction

Ink, bone and one vermilion accent, the red of traditional tattoo flash. Headlines are set in Bodoni Moda, whose hairlines and heavy strokes echo fine-line and bold work side by side; Manrope handles everything that has to be read. A layer of film grain over the page ties the stock photography together.

Five sections, one gesture each

The page has five full-screen sections: Studio, Artists, Services, Works and Visit. On desktop, one wheel or trackpad gesture moves exactly one section. GSAP Observer catches the gesture and ScrollToPlugin glides the page there. Meanwhile the outgoing content drifts on ahead and the incoming content settles in a beat late, which gives each move depth. Headlines rise line by line with SplitText as each section arrives.

Trackpads keep firing scroll events for a second after a flick, which usually makes a locked page skip two or three sections. The lock waits for a short quiet gap before accepting the next gesture, so one flick is one section.

On phones the lock hands over to native CSS scroll-snap, so swipes keep their natural momentum. Visitors who ask their system for reduced motion get the same calm version.

Artists: a roster, not a grid

Six names are set large, like the artist board in a studio. Hovering or tapping a name wipes that artist’s portrait up from below and swaps in their specialty, a line about their work and a direct “Book with…” link. All six fit on one screen.

Services that explain themselves

Tattoos and Aesthetics each take half the screen, with a description and the treatments they include. Hover one side and it widens while a photo surfaces behind it.

Works, then the booking

Past work runs as a slow marquee that stops under the cursor, can be dragged with momentum, and steps with the arrow buttons. The last section replaces the footer email with a short booking form (name, email, service, idea) that validates in place. On this concept site it confirms without sending anything.

Mobile

Each section has its own phone layout rather than a squeezed desktop one. The artist portrait fills the free height with the roster underneath, the services stack, and the menu opens as a full-screen overlay.

Built light

  • No framework and no build step: one HTML file, one stylesheet and one script, plus GSAP.
  • Images went from 6.0 MB to 0.5 MB, and all the code, GSAP included, is about 150 KB.
  • The arrow keys, Page Up/Down, Space, Home and End move between sections, and content that hasn’t revealed yet stays reachable with Tab.
